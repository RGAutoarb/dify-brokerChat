# Risk Management and Investment Safety — Sahel Capital Markets (SCM)

## Overview

This document covers the types of investment risk you may encounter when investing through Sahel Capital Markets (SCM) and the safeguards in place to protect your assets. SCM is a West African brokerage offering 20 selected equities and curated bonds across the GSE, NSE, and BRVM. SCM does not offer leverage or margin trading — you invest only with the funds you deposit. If you want to understand investment risks and how to manage them, start here.

## Risk Concepts at SCM

### Market Risk

Market risk is the risk that the price of shares you hold on SCM declines due to market conditions, economic events, or company-specific news. All equity investments carry market risk. For example, a drop in cocoa prices could affect the share price of Cocoa Processing Company (CPC) on the GSE. Market fluctuations are normal and short-term declines do not necessarily mean long-term loss.

### Credit Risk

Credit risk is the risk that a bond issuer fails to make coupon payments or return your principal at maturity. This is more relevant for corporate bonds (such as the Ecobank Senior Note or Dangote Green Bond) than for sovereign bonds. SCM only lists bonds from established West African companies with strong balance sheets to mitigate this risk, but it cannot be eliminated entirely.

### Sovereign Risk

Sovereign risk is the risk associated with government bond defaults. While historically low for Ghana and Nigeria, it is not zero. Government fiscal policy, debt levels, and political stability all affect sovereign risk. The Ghana Government Treasury Notes, Nigeria FGN Bonds, Cote d'Ivoire Eurobond, and ECOWAS Infrastructure Bond all carry some degree of sovereign risk.

### Currency Risk

Currency risk is the risk that exchange rate movements between GHS, NGN, XOF, or USD affect the value of your investments on SCM. For example, if you hold NSE shares priced in NGN and the Naira weakens against the Cedi, your investment loses value in GHS terms even if the share price is unchanged. This risk applies when investing across the GSE (GHS), NSE (NGN), and BRVM (XOF).

### Liquidity Risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that you cannot sell shares quickly on SCM because of low trading volume on the exchange. Some smaller equities on the GSE and BRVM may have fewer buyers and sellers, making it harder to exit a position at your desired price. Mining and agriculture equities typically have lower liquidity than banking and telecoms equities. Use limit orders for less liquid stocks.

### Concentration Risk

Concentration risk is the risk of putting too much of your capital into a single stock, bond, sector, or country on SCM. If that single investment performs poorly, it has an outsized impact on your portfolio. Avoid putting more than 20-25% of your portfolio in any single equity. Spread investments across different sectors, exchanges, and asset types.

### Interest Rate Risk and Inflation Risk

Interest rate risk is the risk that changes in interest rates affect the value of your bonds on SCM. If market interest rates rise, the resale value of existing bonds with lower coupon rates may fall. This matters if you want to sell a bond before maturity. If you hold to maturity, you receive the full face value regardless. Inflation risk is the risk that inflation erodes the real value of your returns, particularly relevant in markets with high inflation such as Ghana and Nigeria.

## Investment Risk Profiles at SCM

### Conservative Investment Profile

A conservative investor on SCM prioritizes capital preservation and steady income. They are comfortable with lower returns in exchange for lower volatility. A typical allocation is mostly sovereign bonds such as the Ghana Government Treasury Notes and Nigeria FGN Bonds, with a small allocation to blue-chip equities in banking and consumer goods sectors like Consolidated Bank Ghana, Zenith Bancshares, Unilever Nigeria, or Nestle Cote d'Ivoire.

### Balanced Investment Profile

A balanced investor on SCM seeks a mix of income and growth. They are willing to accept moderate price fluctuations in exchange for higher potential returns. A typical allocation includes a mix of sovereign and corporate bonds alongside equities across multiple sectors and exchanges (GSE, NSE, BRVM). This profile benefits from diversification across asset types, sectors, and currencies.

### Growth Investment Profile

A growth investor on SCM prioritizes capital appreciation and is comfortable with higher volatility and longer holding periods. A typical allocation is heavier in equities across all six sectors (banking, telecoms, mining, agriculture, energy, consumer goods), with bonds used mainly for diversification. This profile accepts more short-term price fluctuations for potentially higher long-term returns.

## Managing Currency Risk on SCM

When you invest across the GSE (GHS), NSE (NGN), and BRVM (XOF) through SCM, you are exposed to exchange rate movements between these currencies.

### Currency Risk Example on SCM

You buy 1,000 shares of Zenith Bancshares on the NSE at NGN 35 per share through SCM (total NGN 35,000). At the time, 1 GHS equals 150 NGN, so your investment is worth approximately GHS 233. If the Naira weakens to 1 GHS equals 180 NGN, your investment is now worth approximately GHS 194 even if the share price is unchanged — a loss of GHS 39 purely from currency movement.

### How to Manage Currency Risk on SCM

Add multi-currency wallets under Settings then Currency Wallets in the SCM Invest app. Add NGN and XOF wallets and fund them directly in that currency via bank transfer. This avoids the 1.0% conversion fee and lets you hold proceeds in the local currency. Diversify across currencies so that weakness in one may be offset by strength in another. Monitor exchange rates and convert back to GHS when conditions are favorable rather than immediately after selling.

## Liquidity Across SCM Equities

Not all 20 equities on SCM trade with the same volume. Liquidity affects how quickly you can buy or sell at your desired price through the SCM Invest platform.

### Higher Liquidity Equities on SCM

Banking equities (Consolidated Bank Ghana, Zenith Bancshares, Ecobank Transnational), telecoms equities (Vodacom West Africa, Sonatel SA), and consumer goods equities (Unilever Nigeria, Nestle Cote d'Ivoire) generally have higher trading volume. Orders fill quickly with tighter price spreads, making it easier to enter and exit positions.

### Lower Liquidity Equities on SCM

Mining equities (Golden Ridge Mining, Tarkwa Minerals, Sahelian Phosphate Holdings) and some agriculture equities (Cocoa Processing Company) typically have lower trading volume on the GSE and BRVM. Orders may be partially filled with wider price spreads. Use limit orders rather than market orders, consider smaller position sizes, and be prepared to hold for longer periods.

## Bond Risk on SCM

Bonds available through SCM are generally considered lower risk than equities, but they are not risk-free.

### Sovereign Bond Risk on SCM

Sovereign bonds on SCM (Ghana Government Treasury Notes, Nigeria FGN Bonds, Cote d'Ivoire Eurobond, ECOWAS Infrastructure Bond) are backed by government revenue. Default risk is historically low for Ghana and Nigeria, but not zero. Investors should monitor fiscal policy, debt levels, and credit ratings. Sovereign bonds are generally the lowest-risk option on SCM.

### Corporate Bond Risk on SCM

Corporate bonds on SCM (Ecobank Senior Note, Zenith Bancshares Fixed Rate Note, Sonatel Corporate Bond, Dangote Green Bond) are backed by the issuing company's ability to generate revenue. They offer higher coupon rates to compensate for higher credit risk compared to sovereign bonds. SCM only lists bonds from established West African companies, but credit risk cannot be eliminated. If an issuer experiences financial difficulty, coupon payments or principal return could be affected.

## How Your Investments Are Protected at SCM

### Segregated Client Funds at SCM

Your cash at SCM is held in segregated trust accounts at licensed commercial banks, completely separate from SCM's operating funds. If SCM faces financial difficulty, your cash is protected and would be returned to you or transferred to another licensed broker.

### CSD Custody of Securities at SCM

Your shares and bonds are not held by SCM directly. They are held electronically at independent Central Securities Depositories: the Ghana CSD for GSE-listed securities, the CSCS (Central Securities Clearing System) for NSE-listed securities, and the DC/BR (Depositaire Central / Banque de Reglement) for BRVM-listed securities. This custodial separation protects your holdings even if SCM ceases operations.

### Regulatory Oversight and No Leverage at SCM

SCM is licensed and supervised by SEC Ghana, SEC Nigeria, and the CREPMF. These regulators enforce capital adequacy requirements and conduct periodic audits. SCM does not offer margin trading or leverage. You can only invest with funds you have deposited. This means you cannot lose more than your total investment — your SCM account balance can never go negative.

## Questions About Investment Risk at SCM

### Can I lose more than I invested at SCM?

No. SCM does not offer leverage or margin trading. You invest only with the funds you deposit. While the value of your investments can decline, and in the worst case a company could be delisted or a bond issuer could default, your SCM account balance will never go negative. You cannot owe SCM money.

### What happens if a company I hold shares in is delisted from the exchange?

If a company is delisted from the GSE, NSE, or BRVM, trading in those shares is suspended on SCM. You still legally own the shares, but you cannot sell them on the exchange. SCM will notify you if any of the 20 listed equities faces delisting proceedings and advise on your options.

### Are my investments safe if SCM goes out of business?

Yes. Your cash is in segregated trust accounts at licensed banks, and your securities are held at independent Central Securities Depositories (Ghana CSD, CSCS, DC/BR) — not by SCM directly. In the event SCM ceases operations, your assets would be transferred to another licensed broker or returned to you.

### Are bonds risk-free on SCM?

No. Sovereign bonds carry sovereign risk, though defaults are historically rare for Ghana and Nigeria. Corporate bonds carry credit risk if the issuing company fails to pay. All bonds carry interest rate risk if you sell before maturity. However, bonds are generally considered lower risk than equities on SCM, especially if held to maturity when you receive the full face value.

### How do I diversify my portfolio on SCM?

Spread your investments across multiple equities in different sectors (banking, telecoms, mining, agriculture, energy, consumer goods), multiple countries and exchanges (Ghana GSE, Nigeria NSE, BRVM), a mix of equities and bonds, and different currencies (GHS, NGN, XOF, USD). Avoid putting more than 20-25% of your portfolio in any single stock.

### What is the safest investment on SCM?

Ghana Government Treasury Notes and Nigeria FGN Bonds are generally considered the lowest-risk options on SCM, as they are backed by government revenue. However, they still carry sovereign risk and inflation risk. There is no completely risk-free investment.

### How can I reduce my overall investment risk on SCM?

Diversify across sectors, countries, and asset types (equities plus bonds). Use stop-loss orders on equity positions to limit downside. Invest an amount you can afford to hold for the medium to long term. Read the weekly West Africa Market Pulse report from Dr. Nana Adjei (Head of Research) to stay informed. Consider a higher allocation to sovereign bonds if you prefer stability over growth.

## Issue: Portfolio Value Dropped Significantly on SCM

If your portfolio value on SCM has dropped significantly, this is most likely due to market-wide decline from economic news, a sector-specific downturn, or company-specific bad news — not a platform error.

Steps to resolve: Check individual equity prices under Markets in the SCM Invest app to identify which holdings declined. Review the weekly West Africa Market Pulse report from Dr. Nana Adjei or market news for context. Understand that market fluctuations are normal. Consider whether a stop-loss order would have limited the downside, and set one going forward.

Escalate to Yaw Boateng (Head Trader) only if you believe the prices displayed are incorrect or the platform is showing stale data.

## Issue: Bond Coupon Payment Not Received on SCM

If a bond coupon payment has not appeared in your SCM account on the scheduled date, common causes include processing delays at the depository, the payment date falling on a holiday (adjusted to next business day), or a rare issuer payment delay.

Steps to resolve: Check the scheduled payment date under Portfolio then Holdings then Bonds in SCM Invest. Allow 2-3 business days after the scheduled date for processing. Check Funds then History for recent credits.

Escalate to Fatima Diallo (Bond Desk Manager) if the coupon has not been credited within 5 business days of the scheduled date.

## Issue: Cannot Sell Shares on SCM Due to No Buyers

If you are unable to sell shares on SCM because there are no buyers, this is likely due to low liquidity on thinly traded equities, particularly some mining and agriculture stocks on the GSE or BRVM. Your asking price may also be too high relative to what buyers are willing to pay.

Steps to resolve: Check the recent trading volume for that equity under Markets in SCM Invest. Consider adjusting your limit order price closer to the current bid price. Be aware that some equities trade infrequently and may require patience. For urgent sales, use a market order but be prepared that the execution price may be lower than expected.

Escalate to Yaw Boateng (Head Trader) if you are unable to sell for an extended period and need to exit the position.

## Issue: Currency Conversion Loss on SCM

If currency conversion on SCM resulted in an unexpected loss, common causes include the exchange rate moving unfavorably between when you bought the investment and when you converted proceeds back to GHS, or the 1.0% conversion fee being applied.

Steps to resolve: Review the conversion rate applied under Funds then History in SCM Invest. Compare against the market rate at the time. If the rate difference is only the 1.0% fee, this is expected. Consider holding proceeds in the local currency wallet (NGN or XOF) and converting at a more favorable time.

Escalate to Kofi Mensah (Operations Manager) if the conversion rate applied is significantly different from the market rate beyond the 1.0% fee.

## Issue: Concerned About Safety of Funds at SCM

If you are concerned about the safety of your funds at SCM after hearing news about a financial institution or economic instability, understand the following protections.

Your cash at SCM is held in segregated trust accounts, not mixed with SCM's operating funds. Your securities are held at independent Central Securities Depositories (Ghana CSD, CSCS, DC/BR), not by SCM. SCM is regulated by SEC Ghana, SEC Nigeria, and CREPMF.

Escalate to Emeka Nwosu (Head of Compliance) if your concern is about a specific regulatory matter. Contact Fatima Diallo (Bond Desk Manager) if your concern is about a specific bond issuer defaulting.

## Issue: Bond Issuer Missed a Coupon Payment on SCM

If a bond issuer has missed a coupon payment for a bond held through SCM, this is rare for the issuers listed on SCM but not impossible. A missed coupon could indicate financial difficulty at the issuing company (for corporate bonds) or fiscal strain (for sovereign bonds).

Steps to resolve: Check the scheduled payment date under Portfolio then Holdings then Bonds. Allow 5 business days for processing before assuming the payment was missed. Review any SCM announcements or the West Africa Market Pulse report for issuer news.

Escalate immediately to Fatima Diallo (Bond Desk Manager) who will investigate with the issuer and keep you informed. For sovereign bond concerns, also contact Emeka Nwosu (Head of Compliance).
